## Why Japan?

Japan holds one of the world's most powerful passports, with broad visa-free access across Asia, Europe, and the
Americas. Citizenship is a serious, long-term commitment: Japan generally does **not** permit dual citizenship.

## Citizenship by descent

Japan follows **jus sanguinis**:

- A child is a Japanese citizen at birth if **either parent** is a Japanese citizen at the time of birth.
- Children born abroad to a Japanese parent must generally notify the Japanese authorities within three months of birth
  to retain citizenship.

## Naturalisation

The standard route requires **5 years of continuous residence** in Japan. Additional requirements include being at least
18 years old, good conduct, and the ability to make an independent living (directly or through your household). Japan
places significant weight on integration, including basic Japanese-language ability and proof you can support yourself.

## Dual citizenship

Japan does **not** permit dual citizenship. A child who holds both Japanese and foreign citizenship must generally
choose one nationality (by age 20, or within two years of acquiring a second nationality as an adult). Acquiring
Japanese citizenship therefore usually means renouncing your existing citizenship.

## Fees & timeline

There is **no formal application fee** for naturalisation, though incidental costs (documents, translations) apply.
Processing typically takes 12–18 months or longer, depending on the regional Legal Affairs Bureau.
